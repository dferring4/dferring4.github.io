What Tape Do I Have? VHS vs Hi8 vs MiniDV

Found a box of old tapes but not sure what they are? Here is how to tell them apart at a glance.

By David Ferring · Digitize DFW · Flower Mound, TX

If you have unearthed a box of home-movie tapes and cannot tell one from another, you are in good company. Digitize DFW converts them all — here is a quick guide from David Ferring to help you identify what you have before bringing it in around Flower Mound and DFW.

VHS

The big one everyone remembers — about the size of a paperback book. If it is large and rectangular and says VHS on the spine, that is it.

VHS-C

A compact VHS cassette roughly the size of your palm, used in camcorders. It needs an adapter to play in a VCR.

Hi8, 8mm & Digital8

Small tapes about the size of an audio cassette, used in handheld camcorders through the 1990s and early 2000s. Hi8 and Digital8 are close cousins of standard 8mm.

MiniDV

The smallest of the bunch — around two inches wide, often labeled "Mini DV." These were common in early-2000s digital camcorders.

The quick way to tell

  • Big as a book → VHS
  • Palm-sized VHS shape → VHS-C
  • Audio-cassette sized → Hi8 / 8mm / Digital8
  • Tiny, ~2 inches → MiniDV
